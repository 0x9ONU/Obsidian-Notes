
# Basic Information

- $37,000 annual stipend
- $16,000 cost of education (COE) allowance paid to institution
- 3 years of funding, usable within a 5-year fellowship period
- Total anticipated value ~$159,000
- For research-based Master's or Ph.D. study in NSF-supported fields
- Open to U.S. citizens, U.S. nationals, AND permanent residents (broader than Hertz/NDSEG)
- At any accredited U.S. graduate institution with a U.S. campus

# Eligibility

- Must have completed no more than 12 months of full-time graduate study by the deadline (some extenuating-circumstance exceptions in Section IV)
- Now generally limited to the first year of a graduate program — check current solicitation (NSF 27-xxx once posted) for exact wording, since this has tightened in recent cycles
- Never previously applied to GRFP while enrolled in a graduate degree program; never earned a doctoral/terminal or master's/professional degree
- Covers social sciences, chemistry, computer science, engineering, geosciences, life sciences, materials research, mathematics, physics/astronomy, psychology (non-clinical), and STEM education research — see Appendix in solicitation for full list, and check "Ineligible Areas of Study" carefully

# Breakdown of Submission

1. **Personal, Relevant Background, and Future Goals Statement** (max 3 pages) — must address both Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts under separate headings
2. **Graduate Research Plan Statement** (max 2 pages) — same dual Intellectual Merit / Broader Impacts framing
3. **Transcripts**: official undergraduate transcript required; graduate transcripts can be unofficial
4. **Three Reference Letters** (up to 5 allowed) — submitted directly by recommenders through Research.gov
5. **Application Module**: personal info, education/work history, Proposed Field of Study

# Notes

- Both statements need to work together as one coherent narrative — reviewers are assessing research record and trajectory across both, not two disconnected essays
- Intellectual Merit + Broader Impacts are NSF's two review criteria across everything it funds — learn this framing cold, it recurs in every NSF proposal you'll ever write
- All applications must go through Research.gov — no other submission path accepted
- Deadlines are by field of study and by the applicant's local timezone (5 PM local / stated as 8 PM ET in some sources — confirm exact time for your field when the final solicitation posts)
