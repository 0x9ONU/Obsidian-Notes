## Essay

When considering which graduate institution to choose, I weighed three primary considerations: 1) how closely the lab’s research matches my field, 2) the expertise of the faculty, and 3) how close the school is relative to my family. I am now a first-year PhD student at the University of Cincinnati. Although it is not on the partner list, it is my first pick due to its affiliation with the **Center for Hardware and Embedded Systems Security and Trust (CHEST)** lab [1]. With five partner institutions and 20 industry connections, CHEST has quickly become a leader in hardware security research and publications across both academia and industry. My undergraduate advisors introduced me to CHEST, and my graduate advisor, Dr. Boyang Wang, has helped me begin working in the lab. CHEST has extensive lab resources, such as FPGA testbeds, and industry collaborations that give students the opportunity to take internships over the summer. I hope to continue my research career at the University of Cincinnati and find novel solutions to reverse engineering FPGA bitstreams. 

If the Fellowship were to support a move, I would also consider two potential partner institutions. The Kastner Research Group at University of California San Diego has done extensive work on the security of FPGAs, specifically with securing cloud FPGAs from side-channel attacks [2-3].  Side-channel attacks can be used to reverse engineer FPGA functionality. Signals coming off the FPGA, such as power fluctuations and electromagnetic emissions, can be used to find hardware Trojans in a design. This potential research area relates directly to my interest in bitstream reverse engineering. Additionally, Case Western Reserve University has faculty who focus on the security of hardware. Most notably, Dr. Christos Papachristou’s lab has done rigorous work on hardware Trojan detection and mitigation [4-5]. My IEEE MWSCAS and SATC papers [6-7] focused on Trojan attacks on energy-harvesting networks. This experience transfers directly to Dr. Papachristou’s Trojan work. Attending Case Western would also place me close to my family.
