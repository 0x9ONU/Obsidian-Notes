[[Security in Energy Harvesting Networks A Survey on Current Solutions and Research Challenges.pdf]]  [1] is a survey paper published by Pietro Tedeschi, Savio Sciancalepore, and Roberto Di Pietro that contains various methods to both attack and defend energy-harvesting wireless sensor networks (EH-WSN). After previewing the nearly 250 papers that were cited in the article, there were 15 papers that most closely matched our research contributions. Two of the papers [2], [3] focused on the potential eavesdropping attacks such that a passive attacker can intercept signals between sensor nodes and their base station. To counter the attack, they suggested utilizing a friendly jammer to interrupt the passive eavesdropper or obfuscate the data by using better scheduling techniques. More abundant research done in the area related to DoS attacks, with the most prominent being with jamming attacks [3-12]. Each of these papers focused on physical attacks that added interference to a network by injecting noise to try and decrease the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to ruin the connection and cause networks to either get out-of-sync or allow nodes to die (especially in networks where they *wireless transfer energy* as well).
