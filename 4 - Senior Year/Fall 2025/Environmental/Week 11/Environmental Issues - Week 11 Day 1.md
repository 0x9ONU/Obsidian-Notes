## Water Movement

Water moves through the environment via the **hydrologic cycle**:
1. *Reservoirs*: 
	- Groundwater
	- Surface water
	- Atmosphere
2. *Fluxes*:
	- Condensation (Gas → Liquid)
	- Precipitation (Liquid → Ground via Weather)
	- Evaporation (Liquid → Gas)
	- Transpiration (Loss of water vapor from *plants*)
	- Infiltration (When water soaks into the ground)

```ad-note
Often the **evapo-transpiration rate** is calculated to find how much water is moved from either the surface or plants back into the atmosphere
```

## Where Does Drinking Water Come From?

Most water that we drink comes from **ground water**
- Stored in *aquifers*
- When they are not breached/contaminated, aquifers naturally filter water

```ad-summary
title: Definition
A water table is the top part of the acquifer where the saturated zone and the unsaturated zone split. You need to dig that far down or deeper than the water table to access the water.
```

In the United States, our aquifers are a reliable source of water, *as long as* removal rates do not exceed replenishment rates 
- Need to make sure that the infiltration rates should be higher than our removal rates
- These recharging rates are often in the hundreds to thousands of years
- Can also cause *sink holes* if too much water is taken out, weakening the ground.

## Global Water Use

**Three Main Categories**
1. Agriculture (69%)
2. Industry (19%)
	- Largest use is for electricity production (nuclear, fossil fuels, etc.)
3. Domestic (12%)
	- Toilet (27%)
	- Washing Clothes (21%)
	- Showering (17%)
	- Faucets (15%)
	- Leaks (14%)
