## Developed vs. Less Developed Countries

| Attribute             | More Developed | Less Developed |
| --------------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| Population Size       | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |
| % Growth Rate         | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |
| Birth Rate            | *Less*         | **More**       |
| Death Rate            | **More**       | *Less*         |
| Total Fertility Rate  | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |
| Infant Mortality Rate | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |

```ad-important
Most population growth occurs in less developed countries
```

**Desired Fertility**: The number of kids someone wishes to have over their lifetime
- It is influenced by demographic factors
- It is *tightly wound* with the total fertility rate

**Pronatalist Pressures** increase the desire to have more children
- High infant morality rates tied to poverty
- Valuing children (cultural and religious views)
- Lack of education or job opportunities for women
- Practical help (farms, care of elderly)
- Unmet need for contraception

### Population Momentum

Young populations can continue to grow even after reaching replacement fertility
- Where birth rates are equal to death rates
- These birth rates will outpace the death rates and cause a growth until equilibrium is reached
 
## Demographic Transition

A theoretical model that shows how populations change as a country goes from pre-industrial to post-industrial


| Type of Population | Birth Rate | Death Rate | Population Growth Rate |
| ------------------ | ---------- | ---------- | ---------------------- |
| Pre-industrial     | High       | High       | Stable/Slow Increase   |
| Industrializing    | High       | Lowering   | Very Fast Increase     |
| Mature Industrial  | Slowing    | Lowering   | Slowing but Increasing |
| Post-Industrial    | Low        | Low        | Stable/Very Slow       |

### Reducing TFR?

Reaching *replacement fertility rate* to achieve **ZPG (Zero Population Growth)**

```ad-note
A dip in world populaion growth represents the millions who died
```

**Different Approaches**
1. *China’s One-Child Policy*: Required by the state for every citizen (1980-2015)
	- Caused a complete shift in how the demographics and culture worked for those 35 years
	- Was a two child policy in 2016 and then made it a three child policy in 2022
	- It is difficult to measure if this actually caused any changes (other demographic transition such as farmers moving to the city might have had a greater effect)
2. *Thailand’s Family Planning Program and Humor*: Promotes family planning by putting it into culture by making fun of it all
	- Ex. Free vasectomies on the king’s birthday
	- Sitting around ZPG
3. *Kerla, India*: The Three “E’s”
	- Quality of Life policies rather than just economic policies
		- Education
		- Employment
		- Equality
	- Amartya Sen won the Nobel Prize in 1998 for his work on this policy
	- Why did it work?
		- Matrilineal society
		- No real urban/rural divide
		- High missionary presence across multiple religions

## Environmental Hazards

1. **Physical Hazards**
	- Natural disasters
	- Extreme weather events
	- Exposure to UV radiation
	- Traffic Accidents
2. **Chemical Hazards**
	- Environmental pollution and exposure to hazardous chemicals
	- Burning solid fuels indoors
	- Smoking
3. **Biological Hazards**
	- Infectious agents such as viruses, protozoa, fungi, and worms
	- Water- and vector-borne diseases
	- Spread through contaminated food or direct person-to-person contact

```ad-important
Each hazard can affect one another. Such as floodwaters causing chemicals to spread into the clean water supply.
```
