# Human Populations Day 2

## Developed vs. Less Developed Countries

| Attribute             | More Developed | Less Developed |
| --------------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| Population Size       | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |
| % Growth Rate         | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |
| Birth Rate            | *Less*         | **More**       |
| Death Rate            | **More**       | *Less*         |
| Total Fertility Rate  | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |
| Infant Mortality Rate | *Lower*        | **Higher**     |

```ad-important
Most population growth occurs in less developed countries
```

**Desired Fertility**: The number of kids someone wishes to have over their lifetime
- It is influenced by demographic factors
- It is *tightly wound* with the total fertility rate

**Pronatalist Pressures** increase the desire to have more children
- High infant morality rates tied to poverty
- Valuing children (cultural and religious views)
- Lack of education or job opportunities for women
- Practical help (farms, care of elderly)
- Unmet need for contraception

### Population Momentum

Young populations can continue to grow even after reaching replacement fertility
- Where birth rates are equal to death rates
- These birth rates will outpace the death rates and cause a growth until equilibrium is reached
 
## Demographic Transition

A theoretical model that shows how populations change as a country goes from pre-industrial to post-industrial


| Type of Population | Birth Rate | Death Rate | Population Growth Rate |
| ------------------ | ---------- | ---------- | ---------------------- |
| Pre-industrial     | High       | High       | Stable/Slow Increase   |
| Industrializing    | High       | Lowering   | Very Fast Increase     |
| Mature Industrial  | Slowing    | Lowering   | Slowing but Increasing |
| Post-Industrial    | Low        | Low        | Stable/Very Slow       |
