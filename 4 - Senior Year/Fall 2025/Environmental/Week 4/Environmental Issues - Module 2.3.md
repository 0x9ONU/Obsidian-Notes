# The Everglades: An Ecosystem on the Brink

- The wood stork was listed as an endangered species in 1984 after severe population declines.
- While scientists were investigating the reasons behind this decline, they discovered the entire Everglades ecosystem was in trouble

Florida Everglades and its species provide vital ecosystem services:
1. Capture contaminants and excess nutrients, preventing them from reaching downstream ecosystems
2. Capture and store rainfall, reducing floods from storms
3. Store water and refill groundwater supplies
4. Protect from storm surges

```ad-warning
title: Human Impact on the Everglades
- Altered habitat by draining wetland
- Disrupted nutrient cycles with pollution
```
# Community Ecology

```ad-important
Community ecologists sutdy the many populations that live and interact within a given area.
```

The study of all the populations, including plants, animals, and other species living and interacting in an area.
- Community ecologists investigate how various species contribute to ecosystem services such as pollination, water purification, and nutrient cycling.

**Habitat**: The physical environment in which individuals of a particular species can be found 

**Niche**:  The unique role a species plays in its community, including how it gets its energy and nutrients, what habitat requirements it has, and with which other species and parts of the ecosystem it interacts.
- *Niche Specialist*: Species with very specific habitat or resource requirements that restrict where it can live
- *Niche Generalist*: A species that occupies a broad niche because it can utilize a wide variety of resources (ex. an alligator)
