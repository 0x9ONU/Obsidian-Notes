# Science Literacy and the Process of Science III

## How can Science Help Policy Makers Address Environmental Problems?

They need to address them in a **Policy**:
- *Triple Bottom Line*: Environmental, Social, and Economics
- *Flexible*: As new information comes in, we need to be able to adjust the policy
- *Not very easy*: Tries to balance all factors to the best of its ability
- *Precautionary Principle*: “Better safe than sorry”

## What is Information Literacy and Why Is It Important?

```ad-summary
title: Definition
Ability to find and evaluate the quality of information
```

**Information Sources**:
1. *Primary Sources*: New and original data that is subject to peer review
2. *Secondary Sources*: Interprets primary information and often has no peer review
3. *Tertiary Sources*: Interpret or compile secondary info

```ad-warning
Be careful of tertiary sources as they can "telephone" information that hides the truth
```

### Media Literacy in Digital “News” Stories

What to look out for:
1. The source
2. Accuracy
3. Author bias
4. Reasonably
5. Does the information stack up with other sources
6. *Personal* bias

## What is Critical Thinking and How can it Counter Logical Fallacies?

**Critical Thinking**: Logically assess, reflect and reach conclusions
- Combats *logical fallacies* (which are used to sway an audience)
