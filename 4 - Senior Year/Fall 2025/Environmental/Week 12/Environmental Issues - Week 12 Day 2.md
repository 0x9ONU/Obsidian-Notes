### Malnutrition

Poor health from:
1. *Undernutrition*: Not enough calories
2. *Deficiency*: Not having enough of specific nutrient 
	- (ex. Lack of Iodine causes Goyers)
3. *Overnutrition*: Too many calories
	- Can be caused by **food deserts** (places that do not have access to food that has positive nutrition)

## The Green Revolution

In the 1950s-60s: A global effort to eliminate hunger by improving crop performance
- Increase of pesticides
- Increase of synthetic fertilizers
- Modern farming equipment
- Irrigation methods
- Development of *high-yield varieties* (HYVs)
- Agricultural policies (“Plant Fence to Fence”)
	- Insurance
	- Tax Breaks
	- Government Subsidies

Late 1960s: Moving modern farming to developing countries
- Resulted in a **1000% Increase** in global food production

### Industrial Agriculture

```ad-summary
title: Definition
Farming methods that rely on technology, synthetic chemical inputs, and economies of scale to increase productivity and profits
```

**Advantages**
1. Large-scale farming
2. Higher yields/arce
3. Crops can be grown on nutrient-poor soil
4. Crops have fewer lemishes
5. Crops can be grown in water-poor areas
6. Monocultures are more efficient to grow and harvest
7. Less labor intensive.

*Disadvantages*
1. Dependence on mechanization
2. Large quantities of fossil fuels are used
3. Soil quality is degraded
4. Water pollution and scarcity
5. Monocultures are more vulnerable to pests and disease
6. Toxic residues on food
7. Fewer jobs
8. Decreases the number of family farms, which affects local communities 

### Monocultures

A single crop over a large area
- Often the same species AND variety over a large area
- This causes a loss of *genetic diversity on the farm*
- Higher crop vulnerability to pess
- Lower crop esilience
- Lessened ability to respond to climate change

## The Role of Genetic Engineering in Agriculure

**Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs):**
- Gene splicing from one organism to another
- Put it into a organism to let it gain that organism’s wanted DNA

Types of GMOs:
1. **Transgenic**: Getting genes from another species
	- Glo-Fish
	- Golden Rice (adds vitamin A to standard white rice)
	- *HT Crops: Herbicide-tolerant*
	- BT Crops: Produces a toxin that acts as a pesticide
	- Nutritionally enhanced foods
2. **Cisgenic**: Introduce a gene from a closely related species
	- ex. Dairy cows can be given a no-horn gene from a hornless species
3. **Intragenic**: Changing genes within a species
	- ex. The gene for bruising in button mushrooms has been silenced

### Trade-Offs of GMOs

| Category                 | Advantages                                                                                           | Disadvantages                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Herbicide Tolerance      | Higher yield and less competition from weeds                                                         | Overuse of herbicides is costly. Weeds can also gain herbistic tolerance through natural selection                                             |
| Pest Resistance          | Higher yield from less pests. Less pesticide use overall                                             | Pests can become resistant to pesticides. Risk of the gene spreading to other organisms is also there.                                         |
| Corporate Funding        | Corporations that developed these GMOs are already paid for by the companies rather than tax payers. | The farmer is tied to using products, such as herbicide and pesticide, . It is also copyrighted and the farmer has to buy new seeds each year. |
| Food Security and Safety | More nutritional food and more food for all                                                          | No legal requirement to call out GMOs. Research of on the health safety is lacking, but there are potential long-term concerns.                |
```ad-warning
Only certified organic food is the only way to tell if a food uses GMOs or not in the US.
```
