# Science Literacy and the Process of Science II

## Why are Observational and Experimental Studies Important

**Observational Study**: Gathers data in a real-world setting without intentionally manipulating nay variable

```ad-example
*Hypothesis*: WNS causes bats to awken more frequently during hiberation, causing them to deplete their fat reserves.

Observational Study: Used body temperature to find out when bats woke up or not. They found that the ones without WNS had longer sleep lengths, while the ones who died had a much shorter sleep period
```

**Experimental Study**: Manipulates a variable in a test group and compares response to that of a control group.
- *Independent Variable*: The one that is manipulated 
- *Dependent Variable*: The one that is measured and the one that responds to the independent variable
- *Test Group*: The manipulated group
- *Control Group*: No manipulation

```ad-important
Observational studies are very helpful to help mold an experimental study before causing any problems or hurting bats in other ways
```

### Analyzing the Results

**Statistics**: Finds an observed difference due to the variable tested
- *p-value*: Likelihood that hypothesis would incorrectly be accepted. **You want to minimize such that $p < 0.05$**

## Correlation vs. Causation Relationship

**Correlation**: Two things occurring together
- DOES NOT tell you causation

**Causation**: Shows that one thing directly causes another event to happen
- Experimental studies help to get at cause-effect
