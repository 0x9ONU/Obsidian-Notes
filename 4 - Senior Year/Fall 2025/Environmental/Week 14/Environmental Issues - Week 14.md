# Air Pollution

## Review

- Primary vs. Secondary
- Natural vs. Anthropocentric

### Ground-Level Ozone vs. Stratospheric Ozone

In the stratosphere, the ozone layer is good!
- However… VOC + NOx can combine with heat from the sun and create ground-level ozone
- Ozone is corrosive and is a secondary pollutant.

### Point Source Pollution

- A point source pollutant releases pollution in a singular direction (like a smoke-stack funnel)
- Non-point source is more dispersed (ex. car exhaust and a burning field)

### Smog

Air pollution often results in **smog** in some cities

### Particulate Matter

Suspended particles within the air
- The smaller the matter is, the more problematic they become
- Smaller matter can get across cell membranes
- $PM_{2.5} \quad (< 2.5 \micro m)$ 
- $PM_{10} \quad (< 10 \micro m)$

## Consequences of Air Pollution

1. Environmental
2. Societal
3. Human Health

```ad-note
Air Pollution is not evenly distributed
- Larger cities have more pollution
- Communities with less income often have more pollution
```

**Environmental Justice**
- Access to a clean, healthy environment is a basic human right
- Refers to water pollution, air pollution, etc.

### Environmental Impacts

1. **Acid Deposition**: Secondary pollution can form into acids (Sulfuric/Nitric Acid)
	- Makes soil and the water have a lower pH
	- Plants cannot grow right or grow as well
	- Aquatic organisms can get poisoned
	- Higher elevations are often more affected
	- *Good News*: Acid deposition has decreased significantly in the U.S.
		- Clean Air Act
		- Changes in technology (smoke stack scrubbers)
2. **Trans-boundary Pollution**:
	- ex. 50% of smog in Ontario comes from the US
	- ex. Indonessian beaches have plenty of plastic pollution from other countries

## Indoor Air Pollution

Can also provide threats:
- Mold
- Dust/Dust Mites
- Paints
- Chloroform from hot showers and chlorinated water
- Burning objects
- Pesticides
- Tobacco smoke
- Radon
- Lawn chemicals
- Furniture and building materials
- Carbon monoxide from cars

```ad-warning
Becomes a lot more serious since humans are spending more and more time indoors
```

Developing countries largest exposure comes from cooking
- They might have a fire indoors, which can cause a lot of indoor air pollution

## Air Pollution and Human Health

Links air pollution to cancer, premature births, asthma

**Harvard Six Cities Study**
- Referenced Portage Wisconsin in 1974-1989 as a reference point
- Showed that relative mortality has decreased since 1974

## Strategies for Reducing Air Pollution

1. **Command & Control:** Set a limit and make a consequence for not meeting that limit
2. **Cap & Trade**: Overall cap, but you can trade your pollution tokens with other companies.
3. **Smokestack Scrubbers**: Catches pollutants in the smoke stack to prevent them from getting into the air
