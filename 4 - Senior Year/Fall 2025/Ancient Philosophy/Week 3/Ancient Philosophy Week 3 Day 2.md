# Socrates, Sophists and Plato’s Apology

## Socrates

- Lived from 469-399BC
- It was difficult to find what he said because he *did not write down anything*
- We know about him from people who wrote about him:
	- Xenophon (a retired general)
	- Aristophanes (a comedic playwright)
	- Plato & Aristotle (philosophers)
- There were others who wrote “Socratic dialogues”; but none survive.

### Socratic Problem

```ad-warning
Reconstructing a consistent set of views held by the historical Socrates based on variable and often conflicting reports.
```

In general, Socrates spends his time in the market place talking about philosophy; he affected many people; he was charged with corrupting the yous and impiety; and he was executed in 399 BC.

```ad-important
We focus on the arguments found in Plato's dialogues, regardless of whether or not the historical Socrates himself presented them.
```

### Plato’s Works

Three different periods:
1. Early “Socratic dialogues” (e.g. *Apology* and *Euthyphro*)
2. Middle (e.g. *Phaedo* and *Republic*); and
3. Lae

```ad-note
We will examine the ones in teh first two periods as well as the transitional dialouges between the two
```

- His early period depicts the view of the historical Socrates
- In the middle stage, there are two pillars of Platonism: the immortality of the soul and the theory of Forms.
- In his late stage, his works are very complicated and very difficult to read. Socrates often si no longer a major character and we find Plato criticizing his own earlier views.

### The *Clouds* By Aristophanes

- Plato’s early works defended Socrates and wanted to disassociate him with the “Sophists.”
- Some of the caricatures of Socrates can be found in the *Clouds*, a comedy written by Aristophanes.
- In the *Clouds*, a father does not want to pay the debt he incurred by his son’s gambling habit.
- He decides to send his son to College to learn the Unjust Argument (or Making the Weaker Argument Strong).
- His son successfully defends his father in the court
- But he beat up his father afterwards by claiming what he is doing is just.
- Their neighbors burn down the college.

## Sophists

```ad-note
In the clouds, Plato believed the caricatures of Socrates portrayed in he *Clouds* are inaccurate and are a beter depiction of the "Sophists"
```

The Sophists turned their attention to moral, political and social philosophy
- Included Protagoras, Gorgias, Prodicus and Hippias
- Plato wrote down what they had since the rest of their works were gone (Many of Plato’s dialogues are named after the Sophists)
- They were paid moral educators, who taught many subjects but especially, the art of rhetoric - the art of delivering public speeches.

### Sophism

The public speaking skill was extremely useful!
- If you were a skillful orator, you could persuade people to support the policy you wanted in the Assembly
- Worked very well in the court room as well (Judges and Jurers were not separated)

```ad-warning
The term of "sophism" now has a negaive connatation. It means to use fallicious arguments to win debates.
```

### Man is the Measure of All Things

Plato was hostile to the sophists because they defended **Relativism**:
- There are no independent or objective moral standards
- Protagoras: “Man is the measure of all things”
- Morality, according to Plato, is not democratic. Otherwise, people will justify commit the unjust deeds.

```ad-example
Executing a just person, like Socrates, to death can happen without morality. An unjust action can be seen as a just majority.
```

## Plato’s Apology

- Socrates’s death (399 BC).
- Was found guilty and executed for corruption of the youth and believing in false gods
- Plato was born into a family of large politicians at the time, and would have most likely became a politican.
- Changed Plato’s life - decided to study philosophy instead of going into politics
- Only having the right philosophy could one really find the cure for social evils.

### *Apology* (Defense)

Plato’s version of Socrates’s defense.
- Use Stephanus’s pagination (e.g. *Apology*, 17a) to refer and cite Plato’s works.
- One of the ways in which Plato defended Socrates is by disassociating him from the Sophists
- Both appear to be interested in answering teh same quesitons, such as “What is just?”, “What is good?” or “What is virtue?”
- Their motives differ: Socrates was interested in Truth; but the SOphists were interested in winning the arugemnt and earning money.
- Plato emphasizes how poor Socrates was and that he did not charge any fee.

### The Divine Mission

- Socrates examined a numer of prominent poeple in Athens who had the reputation of wisdom
- He discovered that they were suffering from the delusion of wisdom
- Socrates was wiser than hem int eh sense that at least he is not pretentious.
- In the meanwhile, Socrates offended them (see 23a-b)

### “The Unexamined Life is Not Worth Living”

- The jurors found him guilty are charged
- Both he prosecutor(s) and the defendant, each in turn assess what they think to be the deserving penalty and the jurors vote on the one they thing has assessed the most deserving penalty.
- Socrates could have most likely gotten away without dying had he asked for exile.
- However, Socrates assessed that because he provided an important service to the state by urging people to be good and exposing their illusion of goodness and wisdom, given that he is very poor, he deserves a free meal at the expense of the state, the reward that is usually reserved for an Olympic champion.
- He proclaimed that he will not stop his mission because **the unexamined life is not worth living**

### Death Sentence

- The jurors sentenced Socrates to death (the number of jurors who voted for death penalty was greater than the number of them who voted for a guilty verdict)
- Socrates has no regret.
- It is his view that it is not possible to harm a good human being. You may kill him but not harm him, even if there is no after life.
- Those who condemned Socrates’s death in fact harmed themselves through their wickedness
