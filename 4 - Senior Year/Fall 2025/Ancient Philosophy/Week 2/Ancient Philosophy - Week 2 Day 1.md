# Parmenides

## Introduction

- Promotable thinker
- Changed the way the west thinks of things to this day
- He was able to use logic to challenge how you should look at reality, understand being, and space
- The **father of deductive reasoning**
- Philosophers afterwards could *never overlook* his arguments

## The Poet: How Parmenides Wrote

- Born around 515 BC
- From Elea (his philosophy is known as the Eleatic philosophy or the Eleatic school)
- Wrote in a form of poetry (Homeric type – archaic or dactylic hexameter)
- No rhymes but scans (a dactyl or a spondee). He would utilize six of these *per line*.

```ad-note
**Dactyl**: Is based on the *long, short, short* principle
**Spondee**: Based on the *long, long*
```

- Selected Greek words had to fit these scans, in presenting his deductive philosophical arguments - one of the reasons why his fragments are difficult and obscure.

```ad-important
Scans are used to show feeling based on how active (or inactive) the story is
```

### Fragment 2


#### The Sense of “Is”

**The path of Persuasion** that accompanies Truth: that “it is” and that “it is not possible for it not be”

**The Other Path** is entirely unable to be investigated: that “it is not” and that ”it is necessary for it not be.”

#### The Subject

What is the subject?
- The Greek verb, *ESTI* (a 3rd person singular indicative of “to be”) does *not* require a subject.

**Possible Candidates**:
1. “What is” is (logical tautology)
2. All that exists (the totality of things)
3. No definite subject at this stage of his argument (impersonal subject is used just as when we say “it rains”)
4. **”What can be said and be thought of”** (the subject of Fragment 6)

### Fragment 6

#### Argument

```ad-important
What can be said and be thought of must be; for it can be, and nothing cannot
```

- It is possible for “what can be said and be thought of” to be.
- It is not possible for nothing to be.
- “What can be said and be thought of” is not nothing
- “What can be said and be thought of” is something.
- “What can be said and be thought of” exists.

## Unpacking the Arguments
### *Noein* (To Think)

The third way (the way of mortals) is a confusion that results from the combination of “is” and “is not”.
- Fragment 2 tells us that what-is-not cannot be thought of or spoken; fragment 5 tells us that nothing is “not there” to exist

What about imaginary objects (like unicorns and centaurs)?
- The terms “to think” and “to speak” are used in a very specific sense.

The Greek word, *Noein* (to think), requires and existent object. 
- It connotes primarily an act of immediate cognition. 
- It does not convey the notion of imagining something non-existence. 
- It suggests an act of seeing (with a mind) and ascertaining it.

### *Legein* (To Speak)

The Greek word, *Legein* (to speak) means to utter a meaningful word, which *also requires* an existent object.
- The goddess is, then, precluding the reference in thought or speech to the non-existence (but only what is).

A stark choice between “is” or “is not”. There is no middle ground; the choice is mutually exclusive and jiontly exhaustive.
- Either “is” or “is not”.
- Not “is not”
- Therefore, “is””

```ad-summary
“What can be said and be thought of”, then, either exists or does not exist. But it cannot be nothing because it is something; that is, something is nothing is a contradiction; therefore, “what can be said and be thought of” must exist.
```

### Truth

```ad-note
From Fragments 11.8.7-22
```

If we accept the two ways, then one of the implications is that there is not such thing as change or motion.

A change or a motion implies a transition from what-is-not to what-is, but his is impossible. 

```ad-example
There is a *leaf* that is changing in the *fall*
- A leaf can become green, orange, red, brown leaf
- Green is *not* red
- Red is *not* green
- But NOT-red and NOT0green do not exist; they are nothing
- But if so, a change would involve a transition from nothing to something, which is impossible
```

### Language

```ad-note
See Fragment 11.8.33-40
```

Mortals accept a plurality of diverse objects and give names

```ad-example
- Hot and cold
- Wet and dry
```

Names necessary *exclude* something. By naming something hot, one is also excluding that it is not cold
- The meaning of “hot” requires a contrast”
- If we cannot speak or thought of something as NOT-cold, then “hot” is no longer what we think it is.

```ad-important
Our language reflects a pervasive illusion of the world of appearance
```

### Sense vs. Reason

```ad-quote
(7) For in no way may this ever be defeated, so that things that are not are:
but you, hold your thought back from this route of investigation
and do not let habit, rich in experience, compel you along this route
to direct an aimless eye and an echoing ear
and tongue, but judge by reason the much contested examination 
spoken by me.
```

The world of appearance represents a combination of “what-is” and “what-is-not”;l it is, therefore, deceptive and illusory.
- It is the world grasped by our sense-perception. 
- Our language reflects that world and, hense, it is infested with “what-is-not” along with “what is”
- Our mind or *NOUS* is capable of recognizing only “what-is” and goes beyond the appearance and grasps reality by **means by logic**

### Reality

Logic is capable of capturing the nature of reality, which is unmoving, ungenerable and indestructible (or enteral), one, beyond space and time.
- Past and future is meaningless

### Opinion


Our scientific understanding of the world of appearance is only a mortal opinion and is *not* knowledge; for the world of appearance is not an object of knowledge.
