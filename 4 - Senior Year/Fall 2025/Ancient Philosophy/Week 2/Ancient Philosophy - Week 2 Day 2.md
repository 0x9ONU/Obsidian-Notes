# Parmenides: A Review

## “That which is there to be spoken and thought of”

- If you can think of it or speak of it, it either exists or does not exist
- But it cannot be nothing because it is something
- Something is nothing is a contradiction
- Therefore, it must exist
## *Noein* and *Legein*

- *Noein* is to think and requires what exists; for it connotes and act of immediate cognition
- *Legein* is to speak and implies an utterance of meaningful word (which also requires that it exists)
- The (unnamed) goddess is precluding the reference in thought or speech to the non-existence.

## Change and Motion

One of the implications is that change and motion becomes unintelligible because they imply the transition from what-is-not to what-is (which is impossible)
- But the transition from nothing to something is impossible
- Hence change and motion is impossible

```ad-example
A leaf changing its color: Green (somehting that is also Not-red) changes to Red (something that is also Not-green). But Not-red and NOt-green does not exist. If so, there are nothing.
```

## Language

- Mortals accept a plurality of diverse objects
- They name things; such as hot & cold or wet & dry
- But names necessarily exclude something.
	- ex. Hot implies Not-cold; its meaning requires a contrast
- If we cannot speak or think that it is Not-cold, then “hot” is no longer what we think it is.
- Our language reflects a pervasive illusion of the world of appearance.

## Reality

- Unmoving
- Un-generable and indestructible (i.e. eternal)
- One
- Continuous (spatial uniformity)*
- Past & future are meaningless *

```ad-note
Parmenides is here groping for the notion of "beyond space and time"
```

## Dichotomies

- One vs Many
- Unity vs Diversity
- Principle vs Things explained by the Principle
- Quantity vs Quality
- Knowledge vs Opinion
- LOGOS vs Flux

Parmenides embraces the list of dichotomies (other than Quantity and Quality) that we have examined and deducted argue that the reality of one column and the illusion is the other.

## Q&A

- Nothing cannot change and reality cannot change
	- When things change, they are not objects of *noein*
- He is arguing that reality cannot change (changing leaves)
	- We can only have a belief/opinion based on appearance
- Can the mind only serve reality? Or can language do so as well?
	- Language *cannot capture* reality
	- Language can only capture the world of appearance, which is not reality and only opinion
	- This means you cannot properly talk about reality, since we cannot properly speak meaningful word
	- This means that mortals cannot *legein*
- There is a knowledge that is not captured by language, but there is a knowledge/reality that the mind is able to capture *outside* flawed language
	- The language cannot perfectly capture reality
	- Therefore, language is a defective way to talk about truth
	- He believes that there is a medium that transcends language where you can articulate reality
- To the extent that we are making meaningful utterances, only then are we talking about reality
- Does each person have their own reality?
	- If we all have a mind, we all have access to the same reality
- A painting is also defective in that sense since it can never be done “perfectly”
- How can you explain changes that happen in the appearance
	- It’s not nothing, but it is kind of a mix of nothing and something
	- In principle, you can never bridge the gap between the reality and appearance
- Mortals try to explain the appearance using *science*. He then believes that science is also an opinion and cannot explain reality.
- He thinks a failure to ever bridge reality and appearance
- Is he content in the fact that we will never explain reality?
	- He believes that there is a reality that we can explain one day, but are limited by our way to *legein* or *noein*
- Since science is grounded in evidence, it based on perception, which means it cannot explain reality
- He believes that knowledge is purely *A Priori*, which means it is not based in experience and should come naturally.
- “If science cannot explain anything with science, what is the point of doing science then? Would he see meaning in it?”
	- He said do it if it is meaningful to *you*, but make sure to not confuse it with the true reality
- Imaginary things exists, but he challenges that they are not the object of knowledge and are not part of reality.
	- He sees imagination is part of the world of appearance
	- Mind is the non-physical aspect, while a brain is the physical aspect
	- Imagination uses the brain, but to *noein*, you use the mind
- How can he present his argument using language, when he thinks language is flawwed?
	- His theory is that he hopes if you read his argument, you can grasp his theory beyond language.
- Another possibility is that we do not have a mind, but we just have a brain
	- Skeptic argument: We can only see science to explain the appearance, and there is no way to grasp reality since we cannot *noein*.
