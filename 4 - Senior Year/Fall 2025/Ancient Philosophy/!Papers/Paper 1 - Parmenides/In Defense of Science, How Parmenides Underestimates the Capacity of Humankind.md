## Exposition

Parmenides’ view on science can be deciphered by using six fragments: two, three, six, seven, and eight. He describes the critical sense of “is” that the rest of his arguments rely on in his second fragment. According to the second passage, he introduces a dichotomy of “what-is” and “what-is-not”. The path of “it is" is attended entirely on the prospects of Truth since “it is not possible for it not be” anything otherwise. The other path; however, is devoid of any Truth and could never be learned, investigate, or declared with good intentions (Fragment 11.2.3-8). The sixth fragment, which complements the previous one, declares that “what-is” is an object that is able to be both thought of (translation of *Noein*) and spoken (translation of *Legein*) (Fragment 11.6.1). In particular, he requires that that it exists since both thinking and being are one in the same (Fragment 11.3). He further argues that nothing cannot exist since (Fragment 11.6.2-3).
