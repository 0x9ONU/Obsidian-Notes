Therefore, we have to rely on *two* sources:
1. **Fragments**: Quotations of varying lengths
2. **Testimonia**: Passages containing information about the thinkers *without* quoting

NOT reliable sources. They relied on the memories and did not write anything. Quoting can become paraphrasing. They can be taken out of context and can be skewed

## Where did they begin?

The beginning of Greek Philosophy occurred in he city of Milieus in Ionian Greek in the early 6th century BC.
- A transition form MYTHOS to LOGOS

```ad-summary
title: Definition
**MYTHOS** is a story that attempts to explain the world around us.
- EX. Hesiod and Homer (8th and 9th century BC)
- The traditional Greek mthology. The Olympian gods are anthropomorphic - capricious and unpredictable
- They look like humans, and also are moved by human desires
	- Lust
	- Hate
	- Love
	- etc.
```

## The Switch from Mythos to Logos

```ad-summary
title: Definition
**Logos** is a *rational* account that explains natural phenomena. Also means **rational discussion, theories, arugments**
```

- The world becomes intelligible
- There are uniform and impersonal natural principles

We live in a **KOSMOS**: The world is ordered and we can explain it by discover the underlying principles *without* the needs of deities.
- They believed in many different principles that explained the natural world

```ad-important
This is known as the **birth of philosophy**
```

## Miletus: the “Ground Zero”

Miletus was a *polis*

```ad-question
Why did philosophy begin and flourish here and in the early 6th century BC?
```

Possible factors:
1. Political climate was favorable (there was relative freedom of thought)
2. Relative wealth
3. Written law codes
4. Opened to foreign ideas and were in contact with other cultures.

```ad-important
The foreign ideas were used to challenge everyone's world view, which lead to philosophy to flourish
```

## Thales: The First European Philosopher

He was very popular and had a general reputation for wisdom

Many anecdotes:
1. Astronomy (predicted the solar eclipse of 585 BC)
2. Mathematics and applied Geometry (was able to diverge a river during a military expedition)
3. An “absent-minded professor” (fell into he well while star-gazing)
4. Economics (cornered a olive market by studying the weather and climate)

### What is he famous for? **Water**

```ad-important
**Water** is the fundamental principle that can explain natural phenomena.
```

Look at Aristotle, *Metaphysics* 1.3, 983b18-27

```ad-quote
"Yet they do not all agree as to the number and the nature of these principles. Thales, the founder of this type of philosophy, says the principle is water (for which reason he declared that the earth rests on water), getting the notion perhaps from seeing that the nutriment of all things is moist, and that heat itself is generated from the moist and kept alive by it (and that from which they come to be is a principle of all things). He got his notion from this fact, and from the fact that the seeds of all things have a moist nature, and that water is the origin of the nature of moist things."
```

Two main lines of interpretation:
1. *The Traditional View*: From Aristotelian perspective, he was searching for, what is known as, **material cause**. It is the principle that identifies the ultimate stuff that makes up a given thing. Water, according to Thales, is the ultimate stuff that makes up our universe. 
2. *Second Interpretation*: Thales was responding to the question - “What is the origin of all things?” Everything originated from water.
