# Imperfections

There is no such thing as a perfect crystal
- What are these imperfections?
- Why are they important

```ad-important
Many of the important properties of materials are due to the presence of imperfections
```

## Point Defect

![[Pasted image 20260127170131.png]]

1. **Vacancy**: There is no unit where there should be
2. **Substitutional Impurity**: A random atom of a different element that should not be in the lattice
3. **Self Interstitial**: When an atom is randomly added in-between the lattice (can also be an impurity)

Increases in *temperature* can cause vacancies in a lattice
- Via atom motion from the crystal to the surface, where they join the island

## Linear Defects

### Dislocations

One-dimensional defects around which atoms are misaligned

**Burger’s Vector:** $b$ - measure of lattice distortion

**Edge Dislocation**:
- Extra half-plane of atoms inserted in a crystal structure
- $b$ perpendicular ($\perp$) to dislocation line
- Requires the successive bumping of a half plane of atoms
- Bonds across the slipping planes are broken and remade in succession

![[Pasted image 20260127172735.png]]

**Screw Dislocation**:
- Spiral planar ramp resulting from shear deformation
- $b$ parallel ($\parallel$) to dislocation line

```ad-note
They are visible on an electron micrograph
```

#### How to Utilize Dislocations

Dislocations allow crystals to deform rather than fracture
- This makes the shaping of metals practical
- Heat treatment or cold working can strengthen a material to prevent further dislocation motion (processing)
