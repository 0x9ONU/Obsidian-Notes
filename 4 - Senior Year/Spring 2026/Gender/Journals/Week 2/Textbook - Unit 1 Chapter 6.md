# Intersectionality

```ad-summary
title: Defintiion
A mode of analysis integral to woemn, gneder, sexuality studies. Identities are considered mutually contitutive and can be experience simultaneously.
```

**Single Determinant Models of Identity** should be avoided when considering intersectionality
- Assumes that one aspect of identity dictates one’s access to or disenfranchisement from power.
- Forgets to account of how various cultural contexts shape the identities of the people in the area.
- ex. women across the world share similar experiences, but they are not always the same as it lacks to consider other identities

**Additive Model of Identity**: adds together privleged and disadvantaged identities
- Creates a slightly more complex picture
- ex. a Black man may experience some advantages being a male, but have the limitations provided by his race.
- Exemplified with race and gender wage gaps.
- However, individual experiences differ vastly and for a variety of reasons
	- There will always be outliers in each group

```ad-warning
The additive model does not take into account of how shared cultural ideas of gender and racialized and how they affect the structures of resources and power.
```

## Problems with Intersectionality

- Refers to the mutually co-constructive nature of multiple aspects, but is often boiled down into specific differences (ex. “women of color”)
- Biased towards the United States
- Assumes fixed and stable categories of identity.

**Assemblage**: considers the categories of events, actions, and encounters between bodies rather than attributes
- Empathizes how relations, patterns, and connections between concepts give concepts their meaning.
