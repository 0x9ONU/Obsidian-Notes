# Introduction

The article "Commentary on the (In) Visibility of LGBTQ+ Victims in Domestic Violence Fatality Reviews" by James Rowlands explores how Domestic Violence Fatality Reviews (DVFRs) often fail to adequately represent and learn from the deaths of LGBTQ+ individuals.

# Barriers to Visibility in Fatality Reviews

The author identifies several ways LGBTQ+ victims are rendered "invisible" within the review process:
- **Identification Challenges**: Victims may go unrecognized if they have not disclosed their identity/relationship, if administrative data fails to capture sexual orientation or gender identity, or if the nature of the relationship was unclear.
- **Scope Limitations**: Statutory guidance may exclude "corollary victims"—those killed because of their connection to a survivor (e.g., a new partner targeted by an ex)—which disproportionately affects queer relationships where violence may come from a former male partner.
- **Lack of Expertise**: Review panels often lack specialist LGBTQ+ knowledge. A scoping study found that only three out of nine reviews included an LGBTQ+ agency. This is compounded by a general lack of "led-by-and-for" LGBTQ+ domestic abuse services.

# The "Representing the Queer" Framework

Rowlands applies a framework to categorize how LGBTQ+ lives are described in official reports:
- **Misrecognition**: When a victim's identity is acknowledged but misunderstood, such as treating a bisexual man's orientation as a "binary" status based only on his most recent partner.
- **Minoritizing**: When outdated or medicalized language (e.g., "openly homosexual") is used, or when the narrative focuses on the individual's "openness" rather than the structural context of homophobia.
- **Preserving**: The goal of successfully capturing and transferring the LGBTQ+ experience by recognizing how identity shaped the victim's lived reality and barriers to help-seeking.

# Prevalence and Evidence Gaps

- **Risk Levels**: Victims from gender and sexual minorities are estimated to face the same level of risk for domestic abuse as cisgender and heterosexual individuals.
- **Homicide Trends**: Intimate partner homicide appears to be highest among gay men. However, the picture for trans victims is still developing, and there is a need for better understanding of lesbian and bisexual women killed by male perpetrators.
- **Data Deficiencies**: National statistics often fail to report on gender and sexual minorities. For example, in England and Wales, data only disaggregates homicides by sex and relationship type, making it impossible to identify all LGBTQ+ deaths.

# Recommendations for Improvement

To make reviews more effective, the author suggests local partnerships ask critical questions, such as:
- Whether they understand the size and specific needs of their local LGBTQ+ population.
- If they are taking proactive, inclusive steps to raise awareness of LGBTQ+ domestic abuse.
- If they are working to develop specialist "led-by-and-for" services for the community.
