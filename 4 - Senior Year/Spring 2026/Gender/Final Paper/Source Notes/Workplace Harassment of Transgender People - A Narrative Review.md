# Introduction

The narrative review **"Workplace Harassment of Transgender People" (2026)** synthesizes 63 studies from the past 25 years to highlight the pervasive and often "invisible" nature of harassment faced by gender minorities.

#  Prevalence and Forms of Harassment

Harassment is widespread and often more common for transgender individuals than for cisgender LGBTQ+ or heterosexual employees.

- **High Frequency:** Recent U.S. surveys indicate that **68% to 70%** of transgender respondents have experienced workplace harassment or discrimination.
- **Traditional vs. Unique Forms:** While they face standard abuse like verbal slurs and sexual harassment, they also encounter unique forms rooted in **cisnormativity**:
    - **Deadnaming and Misgendering:** Intentionally using a person's birth name or incorrect pronouns.
    - **Restroom Surveillance:** Denial of access to or policing of gendered restrooms.
    - **Identity Denial:** Questioning or outright denying the validity of an individual's gender identity.
    - **Microaggressions:** Subtle, ambiguous behaviors like invasive questioning about medical transitions or being told to "tone down" their gender expression.

# Key Contributing Factors

The review identifies several structural and cultural drivers of this harassment:
- **Masculinity Contest Cultures:** Harassment is particularly pervasive in environments where men feel intense pressure to prove their "manhood," leading to dysfunctional climates that target those perceived as violating gender norms.
- **Cisnormativity:** Workplaces are often structured around the assumption that being cisgender is the "default," which privileges cisgender employees and renders the unique needs of trans workers invisible.
- **Gender Hierarchy Enforcement:** Much harassment stems from a motivation to "punish" gender deviants and maintain traditional gender hierarchies.

# Outcomes and Impacts

The consequences of these experiences affect both the individual and the organization:
- **Negative Well-being:** Harassment is associated with diminished psychological health and reduced job satisfaction for transgender employees.
- **Organizational Ineffectiveness:** High rates of harassment correlate with lower overall organizational effectiveness and poor employee retention.

# Strategies for Improvement

The study recommends a **multi-level approach** that moves beyond surface-level diversity policies:
- **Top-Down Policies:** Organizations must not only have formal anti-discrimination policies but actively enforce them.
- **Affirming Leadership:** Consistent leadership modeling and organizational norms that disrupt cisnormativity are essential for employees to feel safe.
- **Linguistic Inclusion:** Using inclusive language in daily communication acts as a powerful tool for social integration and improving well-being.
- **Allyship and Training:** Programs that combine knowledge with skill-building and self-reflection can effectively reduce transphobic attitudes.
