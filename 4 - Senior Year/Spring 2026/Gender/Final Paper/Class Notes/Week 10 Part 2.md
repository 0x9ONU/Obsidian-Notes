Mary Thierry Texeira's article, "Who Protects and Serves Me? A Case Study of Sexual Harassment of African American Women in One U.S. Law Enforcement Agency," investigates the unique challenges faced by African American women in law enforcement, focusing on their experiences with sexual harassment. Through interviews with 65 active and former African American female officers in a U.S. sheriff's department, Texeira explores how the intersection of race and gender influences their perceptions and experiences of workplace harassment. The study reveals that these women often encounter "racialized sexual harassment," a form of discrimination where racial and sexual biases intersect, exacerbating their marginalization within the predominantly male and racially homogenous law enforcement environment. Key concepts discussed include intersectionality, institutional discrimination, and the compounded effects of racism and sexism. Texeira's findings highlight the need for more inclusive policies and training programs that address the specific vulnerabilities of African American women in law enforcement, aiming to foster a safer and more equitable workplace.
