# Original Text

```ad-quote
"Come now, I will tell you - and birng away my story safely when you have heart it - the only ways of inquiry they are to think: the one, that it is and that it is not possible for it not to be, tis the path of Persuasion (for it attends upon Truth), the other, that it is not and that it is necessary for it not to be, this I point out to you to be a path completely unlearable, for neither way you know that which is not (for it is not to be accomplished) nor may you declare it."
```

# Analysis of Fragment

## The Sense of Is

**The path of Persuasion** that accompanies Truth: that “it is” and that “it is not possible for it not be”

**The Other Path** is entirely unable to be investigated: that “it is not” and that ”it is necessary for it not be.”

```ad-question
What is the sense of "is"?

```ad-abstract
title: Possible Interpretations
1. **Existential Sense (There Exists)**
2. Predicative or Compulative Sense ($P \text{ is } x$)
3. Veridical Sense (it is the case that... or it is true that...)
4. Locative Sense (it is *there*)
```

Even though Parmenides did not draw any distinction between them, although existential sense seems to be what he is emphasizing

## The Subject

What is the subject?
- The Greek verb, *ESTI* (a 3rd person singular indicative of “to be”) does *not* require a subject.

**Possible Candidates**:
1. “What is” is (logical tautology)
2. All that exists (the totality of things)
3. No definite subject at this stage of his argument (impersonal subject is used just as when we say “it rains”)
4. **”What can be said and be thought of”** (the subject of Fragment 6)
