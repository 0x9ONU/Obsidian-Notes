# Parmenides: A Quick Review

## *Noein* and *Legein*

“To think” is *not* the same thing as “to imagine”, “to see”, “to understand” or other cognitive terms. It is very narrow such that it can only grasp “what-is”. Make sure you are cognizant of the distinction!

“TO speak” means to refer to “what-is” and never refer to “what-is-not”

## Ideas and Concepts

He is not talking about “idea” or “concept”; that is, the mind is grasping or is in “touch” or in “contact” with reality directly without any conceptual medium

## Reason vs Sense

Parmenides agrees that we “experience”, “see”, “observe” changes and multiplicities of the world of phenomena. What he denies is that objects of our experience can be known. The best we can attain is what he calls “mortal opinion” and never knowledge about them

```ad-note
We can have opinions about the world of appearance (including all physical things), but we can come to know only about reality.
```

### Fire, Air, Water and Earth

Assumes the two basic assumptions from Parmenides:
1. Something cannot come into being from nothing (and vice versa)
2. Plurality cannot be derived from a unity

```ad-check
title: His Solution
The appearance of coming-to-be and perishing can be understood in terms of mixture and separation (same strategy as Anaxagoras).
```

- There are four basic primary elements: Fire, Air, Water and Earth.
- None of these elements is prior to another and there is nothing more fundamental than these four.
- They are immutable, indestructible, qualitatively unalterable and homogenous throughout
- Apparent changes are only rearrangements of these four elements.

### Love and Strife

How do these elements solve motion? Two interpretations:
1. They are like Parmenides One: they do not move on their own. So, there was a need for external movers acting on them. Empedocles called these movers *Love & Strife*. The six ultimate factors that explain the world around us, the *four passive elements* and the **two active forces**
2. Earlier philosophers assume that motion is an inherent feature fo the primary elements, so hey were capable of motiion on its own. What Love and Strife then were responsible for mixing and separation of the elements.
## Atomists

Leucippus invented atomism (that is sketched out a physical theory in response to Eleatics) and Democritus developed the theory in detail.

```ad-important
It is considered to be the most successful attempt to explain the phyiscal world
```

### Atoms

Two types of elements: *atoms* and **void**

*Atomos* in Greek means “uncuttable” or “unsplittable”
- Indivisible building blocks, which move in the void and combine to form compounds
- Atoms are eternal, uniform, without any perceptible quantities; they differ only in their spatial properties (size and shape).
- There are an unlimited number of atoms, with an unlimited number of shapes moving in unlimited void.
- Atoms are impassible and incapable of being affected or acted upon

### Void

- Allows atoms to move and to preserve their identity and uniqueness
- Botha toms and voids occupy space and have locations. Where there is a void there are not atoms; where there are atoms there is no void.
- The notions of what-is-not in terms of void is not unintelligible
- What-is may have void outside it, but none within (and, hence, no motion within it).
- Macroscopic phenomena are explained in terms of the behavior of the microscopic atoms.
- All events are explained in terms of the necessary consequences of the interaction of atoms. There are no chance and purpose in anything that happens.

### Macroscopic vs. Microscopic


| Macro                   | Micro                              |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Mutible                 | Indestructible                     |
| Destructible            | Immutable & Eternal                |
| Motion/changes          | No motion/change within themselves |
| Divisible               | Indivisible                        |
| Non-Uniform             | Uniform                            |
| Can include what-is-not | Can *be* what-is-not               |
