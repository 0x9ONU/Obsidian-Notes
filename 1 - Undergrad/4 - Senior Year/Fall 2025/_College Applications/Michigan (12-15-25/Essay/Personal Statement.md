Throughout my time growing up, I struggled with both mental, physical, and geographical challenges. At a young age, I was diagnosed with what is now called Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). It severely debilitated my social, speech, and locomotion learning ability. I had crippling social anxiety and sound sensitivity to the point was only able to make one to two real friends throughout most of elementary school. I was especially clumsy and would often fall over unexpectedly due to my limited coordination. I went through many years of both physical therapy and occupational therapy just to get to a point where I could be “normal.” It was not until around fifth grade where it was clear that this curse was also partly a blessing. I learned that I am academically gifted and can grasp many scientific and mathematical concepts very quickly. From there, it did not take long for me to learn my niche was academics. However, it did not take long for me to hit other barriers along the way. I grew up in a relatively small neighborhood in a below-average schooling system. To learn much of what I knew before going into college, I had to teach myself about subjects relating to my field. The lower budget also meant we did not have “engineering classes” or any technology studies like some of my university peers had. I am even lucky to be going to the institution I am attending now as it is funded mainly off of academic and government grants to continue my education. Therefore, when I had the chance attend a university, I did not hesitate. At ONU, I would find every opportunity possible, which would eventually lead me down the path of undergraduate research. I now write to the reviewers hoping they understand my story. University of Michigan will allow me to continue my dreams and allow me to blossom even further from where I started.
