# Introduction
My interest in computing emerged through independent exploration of both hardware and software systems, particularly security vulnerabilities that revealed how constrained systems could be repurposed and optimized. This curiosity matured into formal training through robotics and programming in high school and evolved into a focused undergraduate research career in wireless networks, cybersecurity, and energy-harvesting systems. Since freshman year, I have authored four first-author publications. I now seek admission to the Ohio University’s Ph.D. program in Electrical and Computer Engineering to continue research at the intersection of wireless systems and security.
# Educational Purpose and Background
Over my time in my undergraduate studies, my research pivoted on multiple domains. Across four projects, I integrated concepts from multiple disciplines to advance both the academic space and real-world applications. In my first project, I sought out how to detect malicious network traffic on a network by leveraging lightweight supervised machine learning classifiers as an intrusion detection system. I first authored two papers during this time as a freshman, with one of them being accepted into IEEE Consumer Communications & Networking Conference (CCNC) conference. In the second project, I focused on the improvement of both sensor technology and analog-to-digital conversion (ADC) electronics using logarithmic techniques. This resulted in my third publication. In the third project, I helped design an interactive STEM learning device for kindergarten and sixth grade (K-6) students to help them teach the fundamentals of mathematics. In my final project of undergrad, I developed various combinatorial malicious attacks for testing against energy-harvesting wireless sensor networks to generate a lightweight intrusion detection system using statistics. For this particular project, it was conceived with the hopes of protecting renewable energy solutions to improve their viability and their prevalence in the energy grid. This project is still a work-in-progress at the time of writing, as I am currently attending IEEE SATC 2026 to present my preliminary results. Then, over the rest of the semester, I plan on working closely with my advisor to publish a journal article. 

All of these different projects have provided me with many skills in writing conference papers and journal articles, delivering both posters and presentations, and working with advisors and other students in research. With all of this being done at an undergraduate university, I am one of the few students who had this opportunity to work on my own research throughout the past three years. My work in undergraduate studies in both research and classwork has successfully prepared me for working in a lab at Ohio University to one day write my own dissertation.
